Dental and Oral · Q&A

What are common dental problems in horses, how are they treated and how can I prevent more?

🚨

If your horse is choking, drooling without chewing, has sudden mouth pain, or facial swelling or one-sided nasal discharge, call your veterinarian right away. This page is educational and cannot assess your horse.

Short answer

Common problems are sharp enamel points, hooks and ramps, wave mouth, retained baby-tooth caps, wolf teeth, tooth loss and gum disease. Treatment ranges from routine floating to extraction or surgery by a veterinarian. Yearly exams, ample roughage and correct bit fit help prevent problems.

Common problems

  • Sharp enamel points on the outer edges of upper teeth and inner edges of lower teeth, which can cause sores (Tri-State Livestock News; UF/IFAS).
  • Hooks and ramps that hinder chewing and can injure the mouth.
  • Misalignment such as overbite, underbite or wave mouth, which wear unevenly.
  • Retained baby-tooth caps, and wolf teeth that may need removal if they interfere with the bit.
  • Tooth loss, leading to food packing and gum infections or abscesses; periodontal disease can cause tooth loss if untreated.

Treatment options

  • Routine floating for sharp points; removal of retained caps during a sedated exam; wolf tooth extraction (PetMD).
  • Veterinarian-prescribed medication or dental surgery; softer diets for worn molars, chosen with the vet (PetMD).
  • Loose teeth in older horses may need extraction (UF/IFAS).

Prevention (UF/IFAS)

Yearly professional exams, a balanced diet with ample roughage, monitoring eating, drooling, body condition and behavior, and correct bit fit. The sources give no specific medications or surgical details. See exam frequency.
