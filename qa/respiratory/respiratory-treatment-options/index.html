Respiratory · Q&A

What treatment options are available for respiratory issues in horses?

🚨

If your horse is struggling to breathe, has flared nostrils, a cough after trailering, a fever with cough or discharge, or food coming from the nose, call your veterinarian right away. This page is educational and cannot assess your horse.

Short answer

Treatment depends on the diagnosis. Bacterial pneumonia is treated with antibiotics, anti-inflammatories and supportive care; viral illness mainly with rest and supportive care; and equine asthma mainly by cutting dust and mold, with veterinary-prescribed drugs. No dose is given here.

By cause

  • Pneumonia: antibiotics, anti-inflammatories and supportive care, chosen using culture results (University of Minnesota Extension). See pneumonia treatment.
  • Influenza and viral illness: rest and supportive care. See influenza recovery.
  • Equine asthma (RAO/heaves): environmental and dietary management to reduce dust and mold is the most important step, and mild cases may be controlled with it alone. Corticosteroids and bronchodilators give the fastest relief for horses in respiratory distress; your veterinarian chooses the drug, dose and schedule (UF/IFAS).
  • Inflammatory airway disease in young horses can fully resolve, with treatment or occasionally on its own (UF/IFAS).

RAO is chronic and needs lifelong management; it cannot be cured with short-term treatment (UF/IFAS). Do not give a bronchodilator or steroid without veterinary direction. See prevention.
